Organisational Structures

How a venture arranges people and reporting lines. Each structure fits a different size, environment, and stage — from a small founding team to a large, diversified organisation.

Source: "Team management and organizational structure" slides by Alberto Zanutto, adapting Mintzberg's configurations

Simple structure

The entrepreneurial start-up: strategic top plus operating core, centralised and directly supervised. Small, familiar, personalistic — suited to a simple, dynamic environment.

Functional structure

Work grouped by function under a general manager. Clear specialisation, but coordination across functions can be slow — a form of the machine bureaucracy, suited to stable environments.

The 5 fundamental Mintzberg configurations

Pure models — real organisations combine them (a hybrid, like a symphony orchestra). Each suits a different environment.
1
Simple structure — entrepreneurial start-up; strategic top + operating core; centralised, direct supervision. Suited to a simple, dynamic environment (small enterprise).
2
Machine bureaucracy — mass production; coordination by hierarchy and standardisation. Suited to a simple, stable environment (car manufacturing, insurance).
3
Professional bureaucracy — high formalisation, low centralisation; coordination by standardised skills; strong professional core. Suited to complex, stable environments (universities, audit).
4
Divisional structure — divisions by product/market; coordination by standardised results; strong middle line. Suited to environments not too complex or unstable.
5
Adhocracy — manages innovation; little formalisation, mutual adjustment, high expertise. Suited to complex, dynamic environments (consulting, aerospace, computing).
