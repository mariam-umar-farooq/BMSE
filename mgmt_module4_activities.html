Getting Things Done Through Other People

⏱ About 8 minutes

The core of management is simple to say and hard to do: getting things done through other people. A manager leads and directs individuals, helping them work to their potential while attaining shared goals — helping and supporting, not just directing and controlling.

Managers by level
▲
Top managers — few in number; set strategy and overall direction; guide the organisation's interaction with its environment
◆
Middle managers — above the first level but below the top; connect strategy to operations
●
First-line managers — supervisors; oversee operative employees directly
💡 The shift that matters for social ventures: from directing and controlling toward helping and supporting — enabling people to do important things in timely, high-quality, and personally satisfying ways.
🤝 Think of the best manager or team leader you've had. What did they actually do that helped you work to your potential?
Concrete behaviours, not adjectives. What did good management look like day to day?

How Organisations Are Structured

⏱ About 18 minutes

Mintzberg described five fundamental organisational configurations — "pure models" that real organisations combine. Knowing them helps you choose a structure that fits your venture's size, environment, and stage.

Mintzberg's five configurations
1
Simple structure — entrepreneurial start-up; strategic top + operating core; centralised, direct supervision; fits a simple, dynamic environment
2
Machine bureaucracy — large volumes, mass production; coordination by hierarchy and standardisation; fits a simple, stable environment (e.g. car manufacturing)
3
Professional bureaucracy — high formalisation, low centralisation; coordination by standardised skills; strong professional operating core (e.g. universities)
4
Divisional structure — divisions grouped by product/market; coordination by standardised results; fits diversified organisations
5
Matrix / adhocracy — cross-functional teams; flexible; fits complex, innovative work
🌱 Most social ventures start as a simple structure — a small founding team, centralised, adaptable. That's a strength early on. The question is knowing when growth demands something more formal.
🏗️ Which configuration best fits a social venture you know (or your own) right now — and why?
Match the structure to its size, environment, and how it coordinates work.
📈 As it grows, which structure might it need to move toward — and what would trigger that shift?
Growth, diversification, or a more complex environment often force structural change.
